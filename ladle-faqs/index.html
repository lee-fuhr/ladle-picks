Ladle questions answered

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

A good ladle depends on your cooking style: stainless steel is durable and oven-safe, silicone is gentle on nonstick cookware, and wood won't scratch but needs hand-washing. Look for a bowl depth of about 3 to 4 ounces for soups, a handle long enough to reach the bottom of your largest pot, and a comfortable grip. This guide answers the most common ladle questions so you can buy with confidence.

What material is best for a ladle?

The best material for a ladle comes down to what you cook and how you treat your pots. Stainless steel is the workhorse: it won’t react with acidic foods, it’s dishwasher-safe, and it can go from stovetop to oven without a problem. The trade-off is that a metal ladle can scratch nonstick or enameled surfaces, so it’s best for stainless steel, cast iron, or aluminum cookware. Silicone ladles are gentle on nonstick pans and won’t clank against the sides. Many are heat-resistant up to high temperatures, but not all are oven-safe, so check the manufacturer’s rating. Wooden ladles, often olive wood or beech, stay cool to the touch and will never scratch, but they are porous and can warp or crack if left in water or run through the dishwasher. Bamboo is a more water-resistant alternative but still requires hand-washing.

How deep should a ladle bowl be?

The classic ladle bowl holds about 4 ounces, which is half a cup. That size is ideal for serving soups, stews, and chili, one scoop fills a standard bowl without spilling. A smaller ladle, around 2 ounces, is better for sauces, gravies, or portioning out batter, while a larger one (6 ounces or more) is handy for punch or stockpot-level serving. Depth matters because a shallow bowl will drip and make a mess when you lift it, while an overly deep bowl can be heavy and awkward to pour. A gently rounded bowl with a slight lip on the pouring edge helps control the flow. Look for a ladle where the bowl is in proportion to the handle length, a big bowl on a short handle is tiring to use.

What is a slotted ladle used for?

A slotted ladle, sometimes called a skimmer or draining ladle, has slots or holes in the bowl. Its main job is to lift solid foods out of liquid, think dumplings from broth, blanched vegetables from boiling water, or pieces of meat from a stew. The slots let the liquid drain away so you don’t end up serving a soupy mess. It’s also invaluable for skimming foam or fat from the surface of stocks and soups. Some slotted ladles have a flat edge that works well for scraping the bottom of the pot, and a few come with a small lip that helps the drips run back into the pot. If you often make one-pot meals or poach eggs and fish, a slotted ladle is worth adding to your drawer.

How long should a ladle handle be?

Handle length is really about reach and balance. A ladle with a handle around 10 to 12 inches is comfortable for most pots and deep skillets. If you regularly use a tall stockpot or a deep Dutch oven, look for handles closer to 14 inches so you can reach the bottom without burning your knuckles. Handle shape matters too. A straight handle is fine for shallow pots, but an angled or offset handle lets you rest the ladle on the rim of the pot without it sliding in. Some handles have a thumb rest or a slight curve for a more secure grip. Avoid handles that are too thin or too slick, they can twist in your hand when the bowl is full.

Can you use a silicone ladle for high heat?

Most silicone ladles are heat-resistant up to 450 or 500 degrees Fahrenheit, which covers stovetop cooking and simmering. However, not all silicone is created equal: pure silicone (no fillers) retains its flexibility and won’t melt, but cheap silicone can degrade or leach odors over time. Always look for a ladle marked as food-grade silicone with a stated heat limit. Silicone is not typically oven-safe at broiler temperatures, the handle may degrade if it touches the oven walls. For searing or deep-frying, a stainless steel or wooden ladle is a safer bet because silicone can soften and become floppy at the upper end of its range. If you only need a ladle for stirring and serving, silicone is perfectly fine for everyday heat.

How do you clean and care for a ladle?

Stainless steel and most silicone ladles are dishwasher-safe, just make sure silicone pieces are placed securely so they don’t flop onto the heating element. Wooden and bamboo ladles must be washed by hand with mild soap and warm water, then dried immediately. Never soak them, because water will crack the grain and encourage mold. For metal ladles, avoid abrasive scouring pads that can dull the finish. If your ladle has a riveted handle, check for food trapped in the rivets and clean those spots with a small brush. Silicone ladles can pick up greasy odors; a quick soak in a baking-soda solution or a run through the dishwasher usually freshens them. A well-cared-for ladle can last decades, so treat it accordingly.

Frequently asked questions

Are wooden ladles sanitary?

Yes, wood is naturally antimicrobial when dry, so bacteria don’t thrive on its surface. The key is to keep it dry between uses and wash it by hand. Avoid using the same wooden ladle for raw meat and then for serving without washing, but that’s true for any material.

Can I use a metal ladle in a nonstick pan?

It’s risky. Metal edges and corners can scratch the nonstick coating, which shortens the pan’s life and can flake into food. Use a silicone or wooden ladle for nonstick cookware to keep the surface intact.

What size ladle do I need for serving soup?

A 4-ounce ladle is the standard for soup, it gives you a full serving without overfilling the bowl. If you serve large portions or want to fill a wide bowl in fewer scoops, consider a 6-ounce ladle.

What is the difference between a ladle and a skimmer?

A ladle has a deep, bowl-like shape for scooping and pouring liquids. A skimmer (or spider) has a wide, shallow, mesh or slotted head for lifting solids out of hot liquid. They are designed for different tasks, though some slotted ladles blur the line.

Are ladles oven-safe?

It depends on the material and construction. Stainless steel ladles with metal handles are usually oven-safe up to high temperatures. Silicone and wood are not oven-safe unless specifically stated by the manufacturer. Always check the product details.

How do I store ladles to avoid scratching my cookware?

Store ladles in a drawer with a utensil divider or hang them on a rack. If you keep them in a crock on the counter, make sure the bowl doesn’t rub against other metal utensils. For wood and silicone, avoid leaning them in a pot because the handle can warp or stain.

In shortChoosing the right ladle comes down to matching the material, bowl depth, and handle length to your cookware and cooking habits. A stainless steel ladle is a versatile staple for most kitchens, while silicone and wood offer gentle alternatives for nonstick and delicate surfaces. Keep an eye on heat tolerance and care instructions, and you’ll have a tool that serves you well for years.
